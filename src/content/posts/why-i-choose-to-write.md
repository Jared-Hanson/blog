Each day, I stand in the shower left arm pressed against my back, hand tucked against my right elbow. My internal dialogue hums with self-described brilliance. What I imagine to be clean, perfect ideas about life rise with the steam, condense onto the tile, and swirl into the drain. I spend the majority of my wash in this state.

The moment I try to express these thoughts in written words, it all crashes down to reality. My web of neurons is in a constant superposition of all my thoughts and emotions. I trick myself into believing it represents a thread of coherent ideas. Yet, untangling and expressing those ideas within the format, syntax and demands of written language has been a significant challenge for me.

Writing is hard. I have to believe it is hard for everyone. I have written plenty in my life, but I could classify the majority of it as a form of human “slop”. My brain just generating the next word based on the requirements in front of me. Essays, birthday cards, emails—just placing words on a page.

The writing I consider hard is that intentional writing. The repetitive review, constant deletion, and mental blocks that come with having to earnestly express what lies tucked in my head.

There are two reasons why I intend to confront said form of communication within this blog:

1. I believe that the exercise of writing by myself, unassisted by AI or peer review, will grow my brains capabilities, enhance my focus, and fight the impulse to have AI replace all my forms of communication.
2. I no longer want my thoughts and opinions to live only in my head or in the ears of those foolish enough to get me monologuing. I will learn to distill those thoughts into concise written content.

I am sure generative AI will appear multiple times within these essays. It is the clear impetus of this project. I carry this overwhelming fear that everyone around me is slowly offshoring all their communication and creativity to these devices. A society worth of communication and art was harvested and distilled into a few hundred billion numbers, then tied together into a layered matrix mesh. It gives us the ability to generate new text backed by the authority and quality of the petabytes of writing that came before. Creating anything manually feels akin to John Henry as he faced down the steam engine. Using these tools is far faster, less mentally taxing and often results in “superior” final product.

So I wont try to argue that AI can write me a *better* essay. The key for me is the AI can not write **this** essay. AI is by nature derivative, it is an average of past works, a stochastic parrot of previous creations. I create these essays myself. The neurons in my head have been forged by my years of life. The process of creating, the things I learn and the changes that are worked in me as I struggle is part of the final product. It is the part of this essay that an AI can not do. A work of creation is as much the output as well as the changes the act of creating  inflicts upon its creator. I no longer want AI to short-circuit that process for me. I don’t want to review a finished output. I want to experience the creation of the work.

So regardless of how I have felt about writing all my life. I choose to write. I write for myself. Not for any audience, payment or recognition.

Until next time
